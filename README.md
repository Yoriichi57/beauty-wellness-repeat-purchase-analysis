## 1. Project overview

This project explores repeat-purchase behaviour in the beauty and wellness segment of an e-commerce business. The business question is:

> Among customers who purchased products in the beauty and wellness categories, what proportion made another purchase in the same category during the available observation period, and what customer/order patterns are associated with repeat purchasing?

The initial analysis uses the **Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist**. Its product categories include `health_beauty` and `perfumery`, and its customer table contains an anonymized `customer_unique_id` designed to identify customers across multiple orders.

**Important scope note:** this is an e-commerce dataset from Brazil (2016–2018), not a dataset from a specific Coimbatore beauty business. It is a public proxy dataset for learning and exploratory analysis. A customer's single observed purchase does not prove that they churned; they may have purchased after the dataset period or through another channel. The analysis therefore measures repeat purchasing *within the observed dataset period*.

## 2. Research objectives

1. Identify delivered orders containing health/beauty or perfumery products.
2. Calculate the observed repeat-purchase rate among customers buying in those categories.
3. Summarize order frequency, item quantity, product spend and purchase timing.
4. Explore whether review scores and delivery experience differ between single-purchase and repeat-purchase customers, where the available fields permit.
5. Establish a data foundation for a later, properly time-based repeat-purchase prediction model.

## 6. Planned methods

- Data inspection: column names, data types, missing values and duplicate keys.
- Data cleaning: parse purchase timestamps, retain delivered orders, translate product categories and join tables using documented IDs.
- Feature aggregation: summarize beauty-category purchases per anonymized customer.
- Exploratory analysis: repeat-purchase distribution, order frequency, spending and monthly purchase trends.
- Future predictive stage: define an observation window and a future prediction window, create a time-based train/test split, compare a simple baseline with logistic regression or a tree-based model, and evaluate precision, recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC and PR-AUC. Avoid random leakage across time.

## 7. Interpretation and limitations

The repeat-purchase label in the starter analysis means **two or more distinct delivered orders containing the selected categories during the dataset's available period**. It is not a definitive churn label. Results may be affected by the limited 2016–2018 observation period, the Brazilian marketplace context, missing product-category translations, customer privacy protections and the fact that the selected categories are only a proxy for the broad beauty and wellness sector.

This repository begins the project with a reproducible exploratory workflow. Do not report results as completed until you have downloaded the data, run the script and reviewed its outputs.
